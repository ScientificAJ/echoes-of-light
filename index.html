ECHOES OF LIGHT

Every viewpoint is ray-traced in the Kerr metric. The disk’s temperature and spectrum are evaluated at the emission time of each light path. Direct and lensed images share one evolving material field.

Interact

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, and tap a point to inspect its ray. Use the time bar to scrub through the transient. Space pauses, arrow keys orbit, +/− zoom, R resets, and H hides the controls. “Lensed echoes only” and “Isolate the moving filament” reveal repeated structure without enlarging its physical width.

Clock and camera

The default compression is 1,800×: one real second represents 30 minutes. An ISCO orbit takes 3.813 physical hours. The observer remains asymptotically distant; zoom changes the optical field of view. Azimuth spans 360°, with elevations from −85° to +85°.

Physical model and limits

M = 10⁸ solar masses; a* = 0.9. Prograde circular orbits in an opaque razor-thin disk from the ISCO to 38 rg. Novikov–Thorne baseline flux, procedural turbulent heating, blackbody emission, combined gravitational/Doppler shifts, and retarded times. No visible horizon surface, artificial halo, jets, or light from inside the horizon.

Real-time geodesics use 32-bit GPU arithmetic and adaptive Runge–Kutta steps. This is less precise and less finely sampled than the offline 6K render. Very narrow higher-order images may remain unresolved. The selected resolution and integration precision stay fixed while orbiting; demanding camera moves may reduce the frame rate. Diagnostic modes are explicitly artificial color maps.

Spectrum

“Extreme UV → visible” uses a fixed 18× wavelength stretch: 21.11–43.33 nm maps to 380–780 nm. “Visible light” integrates the actual visible band. Both use the same CIE spectral response, fixed relative exposure and a highlight shoulder. Background stars are synthetic hot sources. Bloom is an adjustable display effect. There is no sound.
